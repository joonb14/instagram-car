## Selection and visual sequence

Select full vehicle restoration projects. Establish eligibility from original purpose, deteriorated starting condition, substantial work across body/exterior and cabin with chassis/mechanical rehabilitation where relevant, and a documented finished vehicle. Concours originality and replacing every component are not required, but return-to-road servicing plus polishing does not qualify.

Before designing slides, visually verify a photo evidence map: attractive finished-car hero; exterior before/after showing actual restoration such as rust/body repair, paint stripping/preparation and refinishing; cabin before/after showing actual interior restoration; and additional documented major work where available. Each claimed transformation needs its own genuine photos and source-post context. Exterior beauty shots alone, a dirty-car/washed-car contrast, paint-correction prose without revealing before/after photos, or cabin beauty shots without restoration evidence fail selection. Never infer changed seats, repainting or restoration from photographs alone. Do not produce a first post from a partial revival while full-restoration evidence is missing.

Exclude isolated repairs, routine servicing, filter/belt/pump changes, single-component rebuilds, cleaning/detailing alone, accessory installations, wheel swaps and cosmetic conversions. Several small jobs or a striking before/after do not by themselves establish a restoration. A repair can appear as one documented part of a qualifying larger restoration.

Only then choose cases with an attractive real vehicle photograph, documented starting condition, completed restoration scope and corresponding actual after photographs. Rank story clarity, photo quality and evidence over image count. Incomplete projects may remain site articles or collection candidates but do not qualify for the first restoration carousel. Do not substitute a refresh/build when no restoration qualifies.

Order: **hero vehicle photograph → before photographs → after photographs**. Within each group match exterior, interior and detail views where available. Identify each image's stage from source context, not visual polish. Never use a before image as a completed result or invent an after image. If no case qualifies, report the missing evidence and keep the post in candidate status.

Use the approved current logo referenced by the actual site header, with `apps/web/public/brand/README.md` as the asset guide. Historical release artifacts and the old pink FC monogram are not brand sources. Prefer a clean photographic carousel; overlay text only where it adds value. Keep each slide legible alone and inspect the circular/portrait crops as applicable.

## Caption contract

Voice: cool, understated and specific. Summarize the reviewed site article instead of reproducing its narrative. Start with the actual year, make, chassis/model and variant on the first line (for example `1989 BMW E30 325iX`). Put one short descriptive sentence on the immediately following line, such as `Stripped back, rebuilt and made personal.` Do not lead with a separate marketing slogan or birthday hook. After a blank line, use 3–5 short sections covering actual body/paint, engine/driveline, cabin and chassis work. Each section has a short uppercase heading on its own line, followed immediately by a concise explanation on the next line, then a blank line before the next section. Do not use bullets or heading colons. Group related jobs; distinguish a rebuilt engine from serviced replacement units. End with one short result or invitation. Use emoji sparingly for meaning (especially the source footer); do not decorate every sentence. Preserve correct English: `stripped back` means stripped down for rebuilding; do not change it to `stripped black` unless black is the intended subject.

Keep public captions about the vehicle, documented restoration and original credit. Omit production-method commentary such as AI upscaling, generated backgrounds, camera effects, editing software and plate processing. Preserve these details in the private source/derivative ledger and review materials; handle applicable platform disclosure settings separately from prose.

One copy-ready caption: vehicle identification on line 1 → short descriptive sentence on line 2 → blank line → short work-summary sections with uppercase headings → documented result or invitation → relevant hashtags → blank line → mandatory final attribution line:

```text
🚗 {Community} · 🧑‍🔧 {ObservedUsername}
```

Nothing follows the attribution line. Omit URLs from the Instagram caption by default; retain the site link in the profile and preserve the article/original URLs in review materials. Hashtags belong in this same copyable text before the credits. Prioritize the actual standalone chassis/model, make and variant tags before broader restoration and account-promotion tags. For this case keep `#e30 #bmw #325iX` first, then choose relevant car-community and restoration tags such as `#classiccars #carrestoration`. Additional tags are editorial choices, not a fixed universal suffix: retain useful existing tags when appropriate, use `#classic` only when that broader topic fits, and prefer a vehicle-specific meaning when possible. Account-promotion tags are optional and belong last; do not replace standalone vehicle tags with only a combined `#BMWE30` tag. This is the owner's editorial priority, not a claim about measured search volume. Use a restrained number of source-relevant tags. Deliver no placeholders, reviewer labels or separately assembled hashtag fragments in caption.txt. Check Instagram's current caption/tag limits before final delivery; keep substantive prose before credits, with no filler. Instagram's More boundary cannot be controlled. Distinguish completed work, reported outcomes and future plans. Never fabricate an Instagram handle or imply Future Classic performed the restoration. For multiple credited contributors, retain them in the attribution line and evidence ledger. Verify any article link supplied in review materials; for an unpublished article record its status and do not invent a live destination.
