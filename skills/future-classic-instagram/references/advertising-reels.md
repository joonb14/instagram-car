## Learn a visual language, not a fixed shot list

Watch the actual reference and record observed mood, framing, camera travel, wide/detail contrast, approximate cut lengths and closing treatment. Separate observations from proposed choices for our car. Sampling only supports approximate timing. A reference is not an instruction to reproduce its exact order, shot count, duration, setting or parts.

Choose the sequence from the featured vehicle's strongest verified photographs, distinctive equipment and restoration story. Engine-bay shots, wide cabin views, roof/pillar rain detail, wheels, decals and emblems may add value even when absent from the reference. Do not remove a successful original idea simply to match the reference. A 325iX must keep its actual engine and equipment; an M3 reference cannot supply missing mechanical detail.

Before the next film, review recent actual exports and their recipes: opening subject, role order, wide/detail rhythm, camera direction, setting and closing view. Vary the dimensions that improve this vehicle's film. Do not force randomness or make every shot different; continuity within one film remains important. A repeated composition can be justified by the source or owner's request. The same seven cuts or fixed thirteen-second edit must not become the default merely because one reference used them.

Record `mode: "vehicle-advertising"`, source vehicle/article, reference observations, `creativeRationale`, `variationFromRecent`, and per-shot source, purpose, framing, duration and camera movement. Keep history for actual reviewed exports separately from draft plans. Do not log a proposed sequence as an approved or completed film. Run `scripts/review-advertising-plan.mjs plan.json [history.json]` before generation; its repetition warnings support editorial judgment, not automatic aesthetic approval.

## Still composition comes before motion

Build a coherent still set first: the same car, plausible shared setting, weather, exposure and color treatment across front/rear/details/cabin. Prefer originals from the same location when available. Leave more space around a whole car than a standalone photograph needs so camera travel retains both bumpers and visible wheels. Compose portrait from the outset; do not stretch or crop away the car to manufacture 9:16.

Choose a setting from the real source photograph or one suited to the car: bright architecture, a darker restrained space, a green country road, industrial/factory grounds, a harbor, or another justified setting. Rain and wet ground are occasional choices, never a default inherited from the E30 experiment. Alternate light/dark atmosphere and locations across films where useful; do not impose a fixed rotation. Review recent actual exports and record `background: {setting, lighting, weather, rationale}` in the plan. Within one film, keep the selected location/weather coherent across angles.

The E30 lights-off/no-reflection direction applies to that selected treatment, not every future car. For it, extinguish artificial light sources, not just their bright reflections. Reject gold pools, mirror-floor glare and displaced reflections while keeping the car readable. Other films can be bright and dry. Rain outside must not wet cabin surfaces. Inspect each actual rear view separately: front-view approval does not approve rear lighting/reflections.

The owner wants visibly moving viewpoints, not almost static 5–8cm travel. Exterior front/rear arcs of roughly 45–60° / 30° are initial creative aims when source coverage supports them, not mandatory angles or permission to invent unseen geometry. Choose a shorter visibly legible lateral/dolly move when an orbit breaks identity. Review real parallax at the intended final cut length; prompt wording alone proves nothing. The car, wheels and steering remain stationary.

Clarify the actual camera position and path from the latest owner direction. Latest E30 correction: camera INSIDE the cabin, gently yawing right from left-hand steering wheel/instruments across center console toward passenger glovebox. The earlier outside-open-door slider was rejected and must not be reused as this shot. Keep the prior calmer speed; do not equate more visible viewpoint change with a fast move. Deliver a four-second test; if a selected model only generates five seconds, disclose that and trim to four after the desired pan reaches its endpoint. Frame the dashboard and original equipment, not a steering-logo macro. A future explicit outside-door wide view is a separate composition and must not override a requested inside pan. Do not claim cropped components are visible.

## Identity and spending are independent gates

### Cheap motion test before quality production

Start camera experiments at the observed lowest available resolution, exactly one output, with the provider's native duration. Read the live quoted credits; do not assume historical pricing remains current. Keep native five-second clips and trim in editing rather than spending more to obtain four seconds. Inspect actual camera position, left/right direction, pan/orbit versus pullback/zoom, speed, framing and endpoint BEFORE paying for high resolution. A failed 1080p experiment is not a reason to repeat the next experiment at 1080p. Logo/lettering and lighting still need review on the high-resolution result; a motion pass is not an identity pass.

Keep the motion prompt short and concrete: camera location, one move, start subject, end subject, calm speed, parked vehicle. Use accurate source crops and a few essential identity/lighting constraints; do not drown the motion in repetitive prohibitions. Length alone does not establish why a model failed. A centered rear-seat input plus broad cabin framing can conflict with an intended steering-to-glovebox pan; inspect actual input composition and use reviewed start/end frames when supported instead of repeating the same prompt blindly. Label observed pullback as a failed pan, even if the film is attractive.

Every new video reservation records `generationStage: motion-test|production` and `cameraPlan: {position,startSubject,endSubject,movement,speed}`. A test also records `lowestAvailableResolution` and live `availabilityEvidence`; settings must match that resolution and `outputs: 1`. Reconcile its actual job, then add truthful `motionReview: {status,reviewedAt,notes}`. Production supplies `productionOfAttemptKey` pointing to a completed passing test with the same original input hash, model and camera plan. Changing the input, model or motion requires another cheap test. `generation-spend-ledger.mjs` enforces these record gates for Kling web/vehicle-video calls. It cannot see the film; do not invent a pass to reach high resolution. Owner direction currently requests low-resolution testing first; do not retroactively rename old high-resolution failures as compliant tests.

Before EVERY vehicle image generation/edit, prepare separate original-photo reference crops for each visible identity-critical logo, emblem, wheel center, decal or lettering region. Pass these as actual image inputs alongside the scene/vehicle reference; mentioning them in a prompt is insufficient. Matching authentic artwork is supplementary, never a substitute for original-car evidence. Run `validate-image-references.mjs` before submission and audit the actual submitted input list afterward. See `vehicle-identity-qa.md` for records and unresolved details.

Read `vehicle-identity-qa.md` before generation. Recheck steering crest and lettering, front/rear emblems and model badges, side decal text, both wheel centers and actual lamp mounting after every edit and in moving frames. Add authentic matching historic logo artwork as a separate input when appropriate: BMW wheel references only when the original wheel actually supports that emblem; ALPINA steering references must match the original crest. Artwork clarifies a part, never authorizes different parts. Preserve bolts, cap assembly, scale and orientation. Naturally unreadable source text must remain honestly unresolved.

Use the hash-bound input review gate before paid video submission. Inspect start/middle/end and intermediate frames plus playback, then inspect the assembled encoded Reel. Lights, warning lamps, needles, badges and lettering can mutate during animation. A visually appealing still is not a video pass. State any faithful still-photo-motion fallback explicitly instead of calling it a generated orbit.

Reserve every request with `generation-spend-ledger.mjs` before submitting once. Verify current model, duration, resolution, output count, sound toggle, quoted cost and balance: reloads or plan upgrades can change defaults. Keep actual credits separate from unavailable token counts; inspect task history before retrying an ambiguous click. Count rejected jobs as spent unless a real refund is observed. Preserve failed outputs and reasons so the next run does not repeat them.

Finish with the selected film, private source/QA/usage recipe and real Drive delivery verification. Use licensed clean exports; do not erase watermarks. Neither a reference URL, paid plan, approved image nor Drive upload authorizes social publication.
